On the Effectiveness of Hybrid Sparse and Dense Retrieval

We study when combining sparse and dense retrieval actually helps, and find that the gain depends on how differently the two scorers rank the same candidate pool.

Fusion
Reciprocal rank fusion needs no score calibration and is competitive with tuned weighted sums, which is why it is the usual default in hybrid systems. Score normalisation before a weighted sum helps only when the two retrievers are already individually well calibrated, otherwise fusion by rank is safer.

When it fails
If both retrievers make the same mistake the fused ranking inherits it, so hybrid search is not a fix for a corpus with no relevant passage at all. Diversity between the two rankings, not the absolute quality of either one, is what predicts whether fusion will improve recall at k.
